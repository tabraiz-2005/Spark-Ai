# 90-second hackathon presentation

Prepare a genuine completed experiment before the pitch. Do not fabricate results or claim the prototype is clinically validated.

0–15 seconds: “We know we get distracted, but generic productivity advice rarely starts with our actual situation. FocusLab connects a small experiment to a practical next step.” Show the homepage.

15–30 seconds: Show the context intake: “I have 30 minutes to study database joins, but messages interrupt me.” Explain structured extraction, user corrections, and explicit AI consent.

30–45 seconds: Show practice briefly: respond only to the blue circle. Explain two balanced rounds, randomized order, local timing, and simulated notifications. Switch to your previously completed genuine results, clearly stating when you collected them.

45–65 seconds: Inspect speed and accuracy separately. “These are observations from one session, not a diagnosis. Even an unexpected or mixed result stays visible.”

65–80 seconds: Show the AI plan, retrieved source cards, limitations, and tool trace. “The model reads computed metrics, retrieves relevant evidence, and validates the plan. It does not invent a brain score.”

80–90 seconds: Start the timer. “The user chooses the next action. The complete core works without paid services; optional AI uses a quota-limited free tier and a transparent fallback.”

Before the event: verify your Gemini account/model, private demo code, Vercel environment, and live language quality. If unavailable, show basic mode honestly; never present it as live AI.
